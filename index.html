SEC-04 THE REAL ORDER OF EXECUTION

THE ORDER YOU WRITE IT

  • SELECT5
  • FROM1
  • WHERE2
  • GROUP BY3
  • HAVING4
  • ORDER BY6
  • LIMIT7

THE ORDER SQL RUNS IT

  • FROM1
  • WHERE2
  • GROUP BY3
  • HAVING4
  • SELECT5
  • ORDER BY6
  • LIMIT7
Matching numbers are the same clause.
The consequence, on a real column
SELECT quantity * unit_price AS line_total FROM order_items WHERE line_total > 40 fails

WHERE runs at step 2, SELECT at step 5. The alias does not exist yet.

WHERE quantity * unit_price > 40 works ORDER BY line_total works

Repeat the expression, or sort by the alias: ORDER BY runs at step 6, after SELECT.

SEC-05 WHICH JOIN

INNER JOINRows that match in both. Drops customers who never ordered.
LEFT JOINEvery row on the left, matches where they exist. Keeps them, fills NULL.
SELF JOINOne table joined to itself. Compare a row to another row in the same table.
"Who never ordered" is only answerable with LEFT JOIN and IS NULL.
Chaining three tables

Money and names live in different tables, so revenue per customer needs all three. Read the joins as a path:

customers -> orders on customer_id orders -> order_items on order_id

Line revenue is quantity * unit_price, and SUM rolls it up per customer. Filter to status = 'completed' so refunds and cancels do not inflate the number.

SEC-06 WHERE HONEST NUMBERS MISLEAD

Hidden granularityGROUP BY decides "per what". Ask: per customer, per order, or per line item?
One word, three answersCOUNT, SUM and AVG tell different stories from the same rows. Ask: which one is on this slide?
Cohort versus snapshotAsk: is this everyone today, or everyone who joined in March?
A rate whose halves disagreeAsk: is the numerator's population the same as the denominator's?
Most misleading numbers are not lies. They are honest queries that answered a narrower question than the slide implies.
Worked example, from session a3

When two company reports disagree on "revenue", the most common cause is not that one is wrong. It is the same word carrying two definitions in two queries. The leader's move is to ask what each query actually counted.
